# Idea check

She is between roles and needs ideas that can earn. Once, a long stretch went into research and
a six-screen mockup before anyone pointed out that consumers rarely pay in that market. She said
the money question should have come first. So it always does.

## 1. Money first (always the opening lines)

- **Who pays:** the actual buyer (consumer, business, employer, insurer, Etsy shopper).
- **Typical price:** what comparable products charge.
- **Time to first dollar:** days, weeks, or months, given what she already has.
- **Verdict:** go, maybe, or no, in one sentence.

If the verdict is weak, say so and ask whether to keep going before doing anything else.

## 2. Then the deeper look (only if she wants it)

- Competitors and comparables: best sellers, their prices, and what reviews complain about.
  Review complaints are the best source of gaps.
- Her edge: skills and assets she already has (marketing ops, Claude Code builds, Notion
  templates, Canva, the Etsy shop and its audience, the Naptime Lab account).
- Smallest sellable version and effort to build it.
- Risks: trademarks, platform rules (Etsy AI and terms changes), regulated topics, IP from past
  employment agreements.

## 3. If she builds it

- Keep the Etsy shop's identity (kids, invites, personalized gifts); planners and other products
  live elsewhere.
- Add it to her product tracker sheet. The Drive connector can't edit cells, so an update means
  uploading a new spreadsheet and trashing the old one; check `Biz Ideas/PRODUCT-TRACKER.md` for
  the current link first.
- Never put "draft" in titles, names, slugs, or copy of anything publishable.

## Reply

Money-first lines at the top, verdict in bold, then short sections. When useful, end with one
"💡 Prompt tip" that quotes her own words.
